# Why it's called HeyStax

**A haystack holds a needle. Persisted intent is what finds it.**

Somewhere in all the work is the one thing that matters. The customer who wants your idea. The result that proves it. The way through.

You rarely find it on the first try. You find it because something keeps looking, long after you would have stopped.

That something is persisted intent: a mission and a goal that stay held while you sleep, while bots change, and while the first ten angles fail. HeyStax is the platform for persisted intent.

## Persisted intent means hope

Some missions look impossible. Get a medicine through review. Build a house before the permission runs out. Find the second customer for a product nobody has heard of.

HeyStax exists to bring hope to people doing something hard. Your mission does not depend on your memory, your energy that day, or one chat that scrolled away. It is held, and it is worked.

## Why "stax"

Every project is a **stax**: a stack of intent and the work toward it. A mission on top. Goals under it. Next actions under those. People and agents working on it as one.

Today, the judgment on which route to try comes from the brain you bring: you, Grok Bot, Claude or any other agent. HeyStax holds the job. The brain does the thinking.

**Coming next:** over time, HeyStax will learn from the trail which routes reach the goal and which do not. Stax by stax, it will start to make those judgments itself.

## Opinionated, not open

Agent platforms like Grok Bot are very good at what they do. They are open: they will automate almost anything you point them at. They will keep getting smarter.

So people say, "I don't need HeyStax. I have my bots." Plenty of them will be right.

HeyStax makes one opinion its whole design: **your mission comes first.** Every goal, role, worker and next action on a stax is there to serve it, and every one is checked against it.

| | An agent platform | HeyStax |
|---|---|---|
| Starts from | What the bot can do | What you are trying to achieve |
| Holds | Skills, routines, automations | Mission, goals, next actions, roles |
| Bots are | The product | The workers. Swap any of them |
| You ask | "What did it do?" | "Did the goal move?" |

The two work together. Grok Bot can be the brain on a stax. HeyStax keeps the mission it is working for.

## The first user

The White Queen in Lewis Carroll believed six impossible things before breakfast. Colm, who builds HeyStax, has a few ideas before breakfast too.

This is how he runs them. He says "let's do this". A stax is created with the mission and the goal. The Chief of Staff creates the roles. Workers fill them, using their own tools: one buys the domain, one builds the site, one finds people to contact. Each reports back to the stax.

Most ideas find nothing. That is fine; the evidence stays. One finds the needle: the one customer, the one person who wants it.
